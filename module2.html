Module 2: Fake website detection

A malicious site often looks identical to the real one. The address bar is where the truth usually hides, so this module teaches you to read it.

How to read a URL

Reading a web address from left to right takes practice. The most important part is the domain, the name immediately before the first single slash after the scheme.

●●● https://support.paypal.com.account-check.net/login

Subdomain (attacker text)   Real domain: account-check.net

Everything before the final two labels can be invented freely, so "support" and "paypal.com" here are just decoration.

Domain spoofing

paypa1.com looks like paypal.com. The letter l has been swapped for the digit 1.

Subdomain tricks

paypal.verify-security.com is a page on verify-security.com, not on paypal.com.

Misleading HTTPS

The padlock only means the connection is encrypted. Anyone can get a certificate.

Design and content

Blurry logos, broken layouts, missing contact details and pop-ups that demand action are all clues.

Real or fake?

Five sites, one at a time. Decide whether each is genuine or fraudulent, then read the explanation.
