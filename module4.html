Module 4: Best practices and defense

Awareness catches most attempts, but a few habits stop the ones that get through. This module turns the course into a short personal plan.

Six habits worth building

  • Turn on multi-factor authentication everywhere it is offered, starting with email and banking.
  • Verify out of band. For anything involving money, credentials or personal data, confirm through a different channel you already trust.
  • Use a password manager so every account has a unique, strong password.
  • Keep software updated, including your browser, phone and any apps you use for work.
  • Report suspicious messages rather than deleting them silently.
  • Practise regularly. Short refreshers keep the warning signs fresh.

What out-of-band verification means

Out-of-band means checking a request using a channel the request did not come through. If a payment request arrives by email, confirm it by phone on a number you already have. If a caller asks you to log in, do not use the link they send: open the site yourself.

The point is to break the attacker's control of the conversation. They can forge an email; they cannot easily answer your known-good phone number.

How to report a suspicious message

Use your organisation's own process if it has one. If not, this template keeps the important details together.

  1. Do not click links or open attachments. Leave the message in place if your process needs it.
  2. Forward it to your security or IT contact, or use the built-in "Report phishing" button in your mail client.
  3. Note the sender address, the time you received it and what it asked you to do.
  4. If you already clicked or entered details, change the password and tell your security team straight away.
