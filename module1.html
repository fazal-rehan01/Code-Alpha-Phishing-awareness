Module 1: Phishing email anatomy

Phishing emails are built to look ordinary. This module takes one apart piece by piece so you know what to check before you click anything.

What makes up an email

Sender address

The display name is free text. Always read the full address and the domain after the @ symbol.

Subject line

Urgency and threats live here: "suspended", "final warning", "immediate action".

Body and links

Links can show one address and point to another. Hover first, click later.

Attachments

Unexpected files, especially with double extensions, are a common way to deliver malware.

Exercise: spot the 5 red flags

Click the highlighted parts of the email below. Each one explains what is wrong with it. 0 of 5 found

From: PayPal Security <service@paypa1-secure.com>
To: you@example.com
Subject: Urgent: your account will be suspended today
Attachment: Account_Statement.pdf.exe

Dear Customer,

We detected unusual activity on your account. You must confirm your details within 24 hours or your account will be closed permanently.

Confirm now: https://www.paypal.com/confirm This link actually points to http://paypa1-secure.com.ru/portal/login

Thank you,
PayPal Account Team

Sender address: the real domain is paypal.com. Here the domain is paypa1-secure.com, using the digit 1 in place of the letter l.
Urgency and threat: a deadline of 24 hours and the threat of permanent closure are designed to stop you thinking clearly.
Attachment: a double extension (.pdf.exe) tries to look like a document while actually being an executable program.
Generic greeting: "Dear Customer" shows the sender does not know your name, the sign of a mass campaign.
Mismatched link: the visible text says paypal.com but the underlying address is a different domain entirely.

Red flag checklist

  • The sender address does not match the organisation it claims to be
  • Urgent or threatening language pushing you to act immediately
  • A link whose visible text and real destination disagree
  • A generic greeting such as "Dear Customer"
  • An unexpected attachment, particularly one with a double extension
  • Spelling, grammar or formatting that feels slightly off

Legitimate vs phishing, side by side

Legitimate message

  • Sender domain matches the real organisation
  • Addresses you by name when it knows you
  • No pressure to act within minutes
  • You are told to log in through the app or by typing the address
  • Contact details and links match the official site

Phishing message

  • Look-alike domain such as paypa1-secure.com
  • Generic greeting or a name that is slightly wrong
  • Countdown, threat or "final warning"
  • A login link in the message you are asked to follow
  • Unexpected attachment or a request for personal data
